    Where is measurable activity concentrated?

    National Sum of Lights grew +15.9% per year between 2015 and 2024. The light-to-population elasticity across ADM2 units is 1.10 (95% CI 0.80–1.40, R²=0.55): light cannot be distinguished from proportional to population.

    Who lives without detectable light?

    812,787 people — 34.8% of the inhabited population — live in cells that are inhabited but emit no detectable night-time light, against 1,112,504 in 2015. The largest concentrations are listed in the table; they are the natural starting point for a targeted off-grid or grid-extension programme. Depending on the noise floor (0.15–0.5 nW·cm⁻²·sr⁻¹), this figure ranges from 842,428 to 904,286: that is the indicator's real uncertainty.

    How did the lit footprint change?

    The lit footprint moved from 454 to 3,407 km² (+651.0%), with 2,970 km² newly lit and 16 km² losing detectable light. The sprawl ratio of 1.48 indicates a pattern of outward spread rather than densification.

    Is light concentrating or spreading?

    Population-weighted inequality in light per capita across ADM2 units narrowed from 0.586 to 0.300 (Gini). 73% of total inequality sits between ADM1 regions and 27% within them. Initially dimmer units grew faster, indicating convergence.

    Limitations — read before citing

    • Thresholds: noise floor 0.25, urban core 10.0 nW·cm⁻²·sr⁻¹; every 'unlit' figure depends on the first of these.
    • The brightest 1 % of pixels hold 46.2% of the national Sum of Lights.
    • Gas-flare candidates detected: 0. Verify against petroleum infrastructure before interpreting.
    • Night-time lights do not measure electricity access, GDP or population — every such reading is an inference.
    • Activity that emits no light is invisible here: subsistence agriculture, daytime informal trade, indoor activity.
    • No DMSP-OLS year is compared with a VIIRS year; the two are different physical quantities.
